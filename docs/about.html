About Access Atlas

Access Atlas is an independent, non-commercial student project that maps healthcare access across Texas's 254 counties using public federal and academic data. It is not affiliated with HRSA, the CDC, the Texas Department of State Health Services, or any government agency.

What "healthcare access" means here

Access Atlas focuses on one specific, measurable slice of a large topic: whether a county has enough primary care capacity, as documented by two independent public data programs — HRSA's Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) designations, and County Health Rankings' provider-supply rates — set alongside CDC PLACES health outcome estimates for the same counties.

Why I built this

I wanted to build something at the real intersection of healthcare, data, and technology — not just read about it. Instead of a generic app, I wanted a project with an actual problem behind it: figuring out what a designation like HPSA really means, why the highest- and lowest-life-expectancy counties in Texas can differ by 16 years, and how to show that honestly instead of cherry-picking whichever numbers looked most dramatic. Access Atlas is my answer to that — not a hypothetical interest, but a real piece of work I can point to.

What this project is not

  • It is not medical advice and cannot diagnose or recommend treatment.
  • It does not collect, store, or process any individual's personal health information — every number on this site is a public, county-level aggregate statistic.
  • It does not claim clinical validation, partnership with any healthcare organization, or peer-reviewed research status.
  • It does not claim that shortage designations cause the health outcomes shown alongside them — see Methodology & Limitations for why that distinction matters.

Who built it

Access Atlas was built by a high school student as an independent project exploring the intersection of health data, information systems, and public-facing design. Technical implementation was completed with AI assistance (Claude); every data source, methodology decision, and product choice is documented in the project's GitHub repository, including a running log of the student's own decisions and testing in STUDENT_CONTRIBUTIONS.md.
