What Access Atlas actually shows you

Shortage designations, mapped

HRSA's Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) program formally designates places, populations, and facilities with too few primary care providers. Access Atlas shows exactly where those active designations sit today.

Provider supply, by county

County-level rates of primary care physicians, dentists, and mental health providers per 100,000 residents, from County Health Rankings & Roadmaps.

Health outcomes, side by side

CDC PLACES model-based estimates of chronic disease, mental health, and preventive care use — age-adjusted so counties with different age structures can be compared fairly.

Compare any two counties

Put up to four counties side by side across every metric to see how access and outcomes actually differ, not just guess.

Why this matters

A Health Professional Shortage Area designation is a real regulatory classification used to direct federal resources — loan repayment for providers, funding for community health centers, Medicare bonus payments. Whether a county has one, and how severe its score is, has concrete consequences for the people who live there. Most residents have never seen this data visualized. Access Atlas exists to make it legible to the public, not just to health policy staff.

Built as an independent student project. Data from HRSA, CDC, County Health Rankings & Roadmaps, and the U.S. Census Bureau.
